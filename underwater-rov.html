Electrical Design

Underwater ROV

2016 to 2019 Python C Raspberry Pi 3 Atmega328P BlueRobotics

Overview

Designed and built an underwater Remotely Operated Vehicle (ROV) that qualified for the Marine Advanced Technology Education (MATE) International ROV Competition. Acting as both team lead and the primary software and hardware developer, I was responsible for the overall system architecture, electronics design, and control software from conception through competition.

The ROV was designed to perform a range of underwater tasks specified by the MATE competition brief, including manipulation, inspection, and navigation tasks at depth. The project ran from 2016 to 2019, spanning multiple competition seasons and iterative design improvements.

Electrical Architecture

The full system electrical architecture was designed from scratch, covering power distribution, fuse sizing, ESC-driven thruster control, and watertight enclosure wiring. Particular attention was paid to ensuring watertight integrity at all cable penetrations and connector interfaces, as a single leak would result in a complete electrical failure at depth.

Power was managed through a central distribution board with appropriately rated fuses protecting each subsystem. The thrusters were controlled by Electronic Speed Controllers (ESCs) interfaced to the main control board, enabling smooth and precise directional control across all six degrees of freedom.

Control System and Software

The control system ran on a Raspberry Pi 3 as the main computer, with an Atmega328P microcontroller handling low-level hardware interfaces. Python was used for high-level control logic and operator interface communication, while C was used for time-sensitive microcontroller firmware.

Multithreading was employed to manage concurrent control of multiple subsystems, including pitch, roll, yaw, forward/backward, and up/down motion simultaneously. Additional actuators included a claw for manipulation tasks, a winch, a linear actuator, and a mini-ROV deployment mechanism.

Cameras and LED lighting were integrated for visibility at depth, and buoyancy tubes were used to fine-tune the ROV's neutral buoyancy at operating depth.

Competition Results

The ROV qualified for and competed in the MATE International ROV Competition, one of the most prestigious student ROV competitions in the world. Representing South Shore Robotics, the team successfully completed the technical documentation, pilot demonstration, and engineering presentation components of the competition.
